## 1. Position statement

> **Renew You Living is for women who've built lives they're proud of — and are ready to give their own voice a bigger say in what comes next.**
> Through *The Art of Being You* coaching, they learn to tell their wants from their "shoulds," trust their own judgment, and make choices they can stand behind. No starting over required.

Shorter (internal): *Self-trust for women who already know how to get things done.*

## 2. Her situation, in her words (copy hypotheses, not client quotes)

**Where she is:** She makes decisions all day: at work, for other people. But on decisions about her own life, she asks five people, or waits until she's "sure." The life looks right. Something in her has moved, and the old goals don't feel like hers anymore. She's grateful *and* restless, and feels slightly ridiculous saying so.

**What she'd say:** "I have a good life. I just don't feel like I'm in it." / "I don't know what I want anymore — I only know what I'm supposed to want." / "I don't need someone to tell me what to do. I need to hear myself well enough to decide."

**The outcome she wants:** To know what she wants *now*, trust herself on it, and start making real changes (a commitment, a conversation, a standard) without blowing up what she's built.

### Recommendation

- **Secondary phrases to research (all unverified hypotheses):** "life transition coaching for women," "self-trust coaching," "how to trust yourself / trust your intuition," "feel unfulfilled in my career/life," "life coach for women in their 30s / 40s," "women's coaching for identity shift," "reinvention coach" (low fit), "life design" (broad, low intent).

## 4. Three hero options

All three keep her intelligent and in charge, avoid guarantees, and avoid "transform / highest self / unlock."

### Option A — The conversation (recommended)
- **Eyebrow:** Coaching for women who've built a lot
- **Headline:** *You trust yourself to figure things out for everyone else. When did you stop trusting yourself about what you want?*
  - *Shorter version for tight layouts:* **You've built a life you're proud of. Who gets a say in what's next?**
- **Supporting copy:** Renew You Living is identity coaching for women who are done outsourcing their own decisions. Through *The Art of Being You*, a 12-week 1:1 experience, you learn to tell what you want from what you've been taught to want — and start choosing accordingly.
- **Primary button:** Explore The Art of Being You
- **Secondary link:** Not ready yet? Read The Life Edit →

### Option B — The permission (warmest, most shareable)
- **Eyebrow:** Renew You Living · Identity coaching for women
- **Headline:** *You can love the life you've built and still want more of yourself in it.*
- **Supporting copy:** No dramatic reinvention. No one telling you who to become. A 12-week coaching experience to hear your own voice clearly, trust it, and make the choices that make your life feel more like yours.
- **Primary button:** See how it works
- **Secondary link:** Take the Quarter-Life Crisis Diagnostic ⚑ (only if the diagnostic is live and appropriate for her age/stage; see §10)

### Option C — The question (most editorial, most playful)
- **Eyebrow:** The Art of Being You
- **Headline:** *Whose idea was it, anyway?*
- **Supporting copy:** The five-year plan, the "should," the yes you gave out of habit. Renew You Living helps women who've done everything right notice which of their choices are actually theirs, and make the next ones on purpose.
- **Primary button:** Start with the 12-week experience
- **Secondary link:** Read the Life Edit →

**My pick: A.** It names her exact hidden tension (competent for others, hesitant for herself), protects her agency, and is the line you said you most wanted to test. B is the safest fallback for cold traffic. C is best as a campaign headline or social hook; the headline alone is too cryptic for search and first-time visitors without a strong eyebrow.

**⚑ Two-practice note:** Sidney's practice and Louis's (Warrior Rewire) have different audiences. See §9 for how the hero handles this without diluting the women's message.

## 6. Why this direction works

- It leads with **her tension, not your method**: capable for everyone else, unsure for herself.
- It positions you as the guide to her judgment, never the source of answers, which protects her agency and fits your belief that you don't tell clients who to become.
- It uses plain, searchable language in the supporting copy and title tag, and keeps the headline human.
- It sets up the page's job: intrigue, recognition, then a clear next step into *The Art of Being You*.

## 7. Visual concept

**Observations I can make honestly:** your brief describes an editorial world: Gentlewoman-style magazine layouts, art-filled interiors, expressive personal style, candid moments, strong serif type. I haven't seen the board, so treat the below as a proposal to check against it.

**Hero image concept (recommended): "Mid-decision."**
- **Subject:** Sidney (founder-led) or a woman who clearly isn't a stock model, 30s–40s, expressive personal style: a vintage-looking blazer worn off the shoulder, one interesting piece of jewelry, unfussy hair.
- **Action:** Mid-gesture, laughing or mid-sentence, as if she's just said something to someone off-frame. Not meditating, not staring out a window, no mug.
- **Environment:** A real, art-filled room: a worn velvet chair, books stacked sideways, a contemporary painting leaning rather than hung, flowers in a mismatched vase. Lived-in, not staged.
- **Composition:** Portrait-oriented, subject off-center (right third), generous negative space on the left for the headline. One slightly out-of-focus foreground object (a hand, a chair arm) for depth. Crop slightly tighter than comfortable.
- **Lighting:** Warm window light, one side, soft shadow on the other. Slight film-like grain.
- **Colors:** Deep aubergine and ivory as base; one saturated accent in the scene (a tomato-red object or butter-yellow wall), so the photo and the page "match" without matching exactly.
- **Emotional intent:** Intrigue and warmth. "I'd like to be in that conversation."

**Palette hierarchy (restrained):**

| Role | Color | Use | Contrast note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas | Warm ivory `#F7F0E5` | Backgrounds | |
| Text/structure | Deep aubergine `#422D42` | Headlines, body, buttons | 11.0:1 on ivory ✓ |
| Accent 1 | Tomato `#C84C3F` | Links, underlines, one CTA at a time | 4.07:1 on ivory: OK for large text/UI only, **not body text** |
| Block color | Powder blue `#B9D2EB`, petal pink `#E9A7B1`, butter `#E8CD76` | Section backgrounds, never text | Aubergine on each: 8.0 / 6.3 / 7.96 ✓ |

Your earlier exploratory colors (petrol teal, dark cherry, lilac, orange, chartreuse, olive) read as a **moodier, more saturated** world than the six hexes in the brief. ⚑ Decide which direction you want. If the board is moodier, petrol teal or dark cherry could replace aubergine as the text/structure color. Either way: **one dominant dark, one canvas, one accent per section.**

## 8. Layout and responsive behavior

**Desktop (≥1024px):**
- Full-width split. Left 5/12 on ivory: eyebrow (small caps), headline in a characterful serif at ~64–88px, supporting copy in a clean sans at 18–20px, button + text link. Right 7/12: full-bleed portrait, bleeding off the top and right edge.
- Thin rule and a small handwritten-style annotation near the photo ("exhibit A: the five-year plan"), at most one.
- Sticky nav: Home · The Art of Being You · The Life Edit · About · [Apply/Inquire button].
- Don't put the headline over the photo, which avoids contrast problems.

**Mobile (≤640px):**
- Stack: eyebrow → headline (36–44px) → photo cropped 4:5 → supporting copy → full-width button → text link. Keep headline above the fold; the button within the first scroll.
- Shorten the headline to the *"Who gets a say in what's next?"* version.
- Serve a smaller, WebP/AVIF hero; set explicit dimensions; eager-load only the hero image (target LCP < 2.5s).
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; no autoplay.

**Accessibility:** one `<h1>` (the headline), button text with 4.5:1+ contrast, visible focus states, ≥44px touch targets.

3. **What exactly can the button lead to today?** Apply, book a call, buy, or diagnostic. The CTA must match what's actually available.
4. **Is the Quarter-Life Crisis Diagnostic live and the right entry point?** The name implies a younger audience than "30s to early 40s."
5. **Search volume for "identity coaching for women"** (and alternatives).
6. **Is Sidney the hero image, or a range of women?** Founder-led (my recommendation) or casting changes the concept and the shot list.
7. **Colors:** the brief's six hexes vs. the moodier exploratory palette (teal, cherry, lilac, olive).
8. **Approved client stories and any outcome language.** Without them, the hero should make no result claims, which this draft doesn't.
9. **"Identity coaching" as a label.** Is it right for how you want to be known, or too clinical? Alternatives: "self-trust coaching," "life transition coaching."
